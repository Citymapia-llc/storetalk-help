title: "Set up Sign in with WhatsApp"
description: "Register your website or app in Settings, get its client ID and secret, and manage limits and countries."
---

You manage Sign in with WhatsApp in **Settings → Sign in with WhatsApp** (under *Store*).

<Note>
Only the **Owner** and **Admins** can manage this page by default (permission: *Manage Sign in with
WhatsApp*). Other team members don't see it.
</Note>

## Before you start

- Your **own WhatsApp Business account** is connected under **Settings → WhatsApp**.
- You're on a **paid plan** or an active **free trial**.

If something is missing, the page tells you what and links to the right place.

## 1. Check the code template

StoreTalk sends codes using a WhatsApp **authentication template** on your own account. It's created for
you automatically (the first time you add a website) and Meta usually approves it within minutes.

| Status | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **Ready — codes are sent from your WhatsApp number** | Customers can sign in. |
| **Waiting for Meta to approve your code template** | Customers see *“Sign-in is being set up for this business”* until Meta approves it. |

## 2. Add your website or app

<Steps>
  <Step title="Click Add">
    In **Websites and apps**, click **Add**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name it">
    Use a name customers will recognise, e.g. *Citymapia website*. It's shown on the sign-in page
    (*“Sign in to Citymapia website”*).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the type">
    - **Website with a server** — your server finishes the sign-in using a **client secret**
      (WordPress, Next.js, Laravel, Node, PHP…). Choose this if you're unsure.
    - **Browser or mobile app** — single-page web apps and iOS/Android apps. No secret; they use a
      one-time security check (PKCE) instead. You can't change the type later.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add redirect URIs">
    The address(es) where customers return after signing in, one per line — your developer will give you
    these, e.g. `https://example.com/auth/callback`. They must match **exactly**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set countries and limits (optional)">
    See [Countries and limits](#countries-and-limits) below. The defaults are fine to start.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the details">
    You'll see the **Client ID**, the **Client secret** (website with a server only) and the
    **Discovery URL**. Copy them and give them to your developer.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
The **client secret is shown only once**. Store it somewhere safe (like your hosting provider's
environment settings). If it's lost, create a new one — see [Secrets](#secrets).
</Warning>

### Redirect URI rules

- Must start with `https://` — or `http://localhost` while testing on a computer.
- Mobile apps can use an app link like `https://example.com/auth/callback` (recommended) or an app scheme
  like `com.example.app:/oauth`.
- No `#` part, and up to 10 per website or app.

### Allowed origins (browser apps only)

If a **browser app** finishes the sign-in from the web page itself, add the site's address here, e.g.
`https://example.com` (no path). Websites with a server don't need this.

## Countries and limits

| Setting | Default | What it does |
|---------|---------|--------------|
| **Countries** | Your WhatsApp number's country | Calling codes customers may sign in from, e.g. `91, 44`. Numbers from other countries are refused. |
| **Codes per day** | 200 | The most codes this website can send in a day (1–5,000). Protects your Meta bill if someone tries to abuse your sign-in page. |
| **Stay signed in (days)** | 30 | How long customers can use **Continue as …** without a new code (1–90). It extends each time they use it. |

<Tip>
International authentication messages cost much more than domestic ones and are the usual target of
abuse. Only add countries your customers actually come from.
</Tip>

## Usage

Once you have a website, the page shows the **last 30 days**:

- **Codes sent** — codes sent to customers' WhatsApp.
- **Sign-ins** — sign-ins with a code plus quick **Continue as …** sign-ins.
- **Blocked** — attempts stopped by the security checks (too many requests, bot check, wrong country…).
- **Meta charges (est.)** — an estimate of what Meta charges your WhatsApp account for the codes.

## Managing a website or app

Each website or app card shows its status, client ID, redirect URIs, countries, limits and last sign-in.

| Action | What happens |
|--------|--------------|
| **Edit** | Change the name, redirect URIs, origins, countries and limits. |
| **New secret** | Creates a new client secret (websites with a server). The old one keeps working for **24 hours** so you can update your website without downtime. |
| **Turn off / Turn on** | Pauses sign-ins for this website without deleting it. |
| **Remove** | Deletes it. Customers can no longer sign in to it, and their saved consents for it are removed. |

### Statuses

| Status | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **Active** | Customers can sign in. |
| **Turned off** | You paused it. |
| **Suspended** | Your own WhatsApp account isn't connected or your plan isn't active. Fix that and it resumes on its own. |

## Secrets

- Treat the client secret like a password: keep it on your server only, never in website code or apps.
- If it may have leaked, click **New secret**, update your website, and the old one stops working after 24 hours.
- Browser and mobile apps don't have a secret.

## Contacts

Every customer who signs in is added to (or matched in) **Contacts** by their WhatsApp number, so you can
see them in the inbox, segment them and include them in campaigns.

<Note>
Signing in doesn't subscribe anyone to marketing, and it never changes a contact's opt-out. The first time
customers sign in they can tick an optional, unticked box to receive offers from you — see
[What customers see](/sign-in-with-whatsapp/customer-experience).
</Note>
